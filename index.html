NASA's Landsat program has spent over fifty years photographing the Earth from orbit — closely enough to pick out a single river bend, far enough to see a whole desert breathe. Somewhere in that fifty-year archive, six scenes line up to spell your name.

So this is your name, in the Earth's own handwriting. Scroll through the flight log below — each letter is a real place, and each one got a line of its own.

A river that refuses to pick one channel, so it carves a dozen and keeps all of them. May this year let you wander a little — every bend still counts as progress.

An oxbow is the river's old path, abandoned on purpose for a straighter one. Not every door you've closed this year was a loss — some were just the river finding its better line.

One green line, irrigated straight through an ocean of sand, because somebody decided the desert didn't get a vote. May your next year be exactly that stubborn.

Molten rock, photographed mid-argument with a glacier. Whatever this year throws something cold at you — may you still be the one running hot.

However this year unfolds — happy birthday, Shanti.
